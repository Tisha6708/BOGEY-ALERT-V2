BOGEY-ALERT SECURITY POLICIES

POLICY 1 — DEVELOPER ROLE
Developers are normally permitted to create, modify, and move development files.
Developers are not normally permitted to delete restricted company data.
Developers are not permitted to access sensitive company data unless explicit authorization has been granted.

POLICY 2 — SENSITIVE DATA
Sensitive company files must only be accessed for legitimate business purposes.
Bulk creation, modification, deletion, or movement of sensitive files should be treated as potentially suspicious.
Any approved sensitive-data operation must have valid authorization from an appropriate security administrator or manager.

POLICY 3 — AFTER-HOURS ACTIVITY
Normal working hours for employees are 09:00 to 18:00 local organizational time.
Activity outside normal working hours should be investigated when it represents a significant deviation from the employee's normal behavior.

POLICY 4 — WEEKEND ACTIVITY
Weekend activity is not normally permitted for standard developer roles.
Approved weekend work must have explicit authorization.

POLICY 5 — DATA VOLUME
Large unexpected data volumes should be investigated when they significantly exceed an employee's normal behavioral baseline.
High data volume combined with sensitive-file activity or unusual file operations represents increased security risk.

POLICY 6 — MASS FILE OPERATIONS
Mass deletion, modification, creation, or movement of files should be investigated when it significantly deviates from normal employee behavior.
The security system must distinguish between authorized operational activity and unexplained anomalous activity.

POLICY 7 — AUTHORIZATION
An approved authorization does not disable monitoring or machine-learning detection.
Authorized activity should remain visible to analysts but may reduce the associated risk when the authorization explicitly covers the observed activity.

POLICY 8 — HUMAN REVIEW
BOGEY-ALERT provides security analysts with evidence, behavioral analysis, policy context, and AI-assisted explanations.
The AI system must not make the final security decision.
Final decisions remain with the authorized security analyst.
